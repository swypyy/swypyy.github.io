CHAPTER 1: The First Nightmare

The TV didn’t just static—it flatlined.
A sharp, high-pitched hum pierced the quiet living room. Sixteen-year-old Leo dropped the remote, frowning. The screen was completely black, stripped of all signal. He stood up, muttered under his breath, and crouched down behind the entertainment center to check the power and signal cables.
Rrrrrring.
The house phone on the coffee table blared.
Leo jumped, hitting his head hard against the wooden shelf. Rubbing his forehead, he stood up and picked up the receiver. Before he could even say a word, a voice rushed through the speaker—frantic, breathless, and terrifyingly close.
"Just hear me out. Do not open the front door. If someone knocks, if someone horns... never open it. Please—"
Thud.
The line went dead.
Leo stared at the plastic receiver in his hand. His heart hammered against his ribs. "What kind of sick prank is this?" he whispered to the empty house. His parents weren't home. He was completely alone.
Suddenly, the overhead lights flickered once, twice, and died. Total, suffocating darkness swallowed the room.
Before Leo could catch his breath, a heavy knock echoed from the front door.
Thump. Thump. Thump.
Frozen with fear, Leo crept toward the front window. Outside, the night was pitch black, save for the flickering glow of the street lamp. At first, he saw nothing. But as his eyes adjusted, his blood ran cold.
Standing right in the center of the light was a silhouette. It looked human, but it had no face, no features—just a shifting, ink-black shadow that flickered in sync with the dying street lamp.
Blink. Blink.
With a final electrical pop, the street lamp died completely. The figure vanished into the dark.
At that exact second, a terrifying crash echoed from upstairs—the sound of heavy things falling down, followed by slow, dragging footsteps.
Panic clawed at Leo’s throat. He couldn't go upstairs. He needed light. Scrambling in the dark, he found the hallway breaker board and shoved the main switch back up.
The lights flooded the house with harsh, bright electricity.
Rrrrrring.
The phone started ringing again.
Trembling, Leo backed away, his eyes locked on the telephone. He walked close and picked it up. Before he could even say a word, the voice on the other end started speaking. Exactly the same words. Exactly the same panic.
"Just hear me out..."
Leo snapped, "Hello?! Who is this—"
The voice didn't stop. It rolled right over his words, finishing the exact same sentence before the line cut out with a dull thud.
Leo stared at the phone. Is the light going to...
Right on cue, the overhead bulb flickered and died. Total darkness.
A heavy knock hammered against the front door. Thump. Thump. Thump.
Fear boiled over into blind anger and exhaustion. Leo didn't wait this time. He stormed straight to the main board, slammed the switch up, and flooded the house with light. Grabbing his cell phone, he furiously dialed his parents. Ring... ring... ring. Nothing. No answer.
"Pick up, damn it!"
He couldn't take the waiting anymore. Driven by a surge of desperate defiance, Leo marched to the front door and threw it wide open.
He didn't see a street lamp. He didn't see a shadow.
Pitch-black, suffocating darkness swallowed him whole.
Leo screamed—a raw, tearing sound—before the world violently went blank.
